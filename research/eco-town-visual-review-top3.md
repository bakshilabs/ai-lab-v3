# Eco Town: video and visual review of the three best-selling town builders

Reviewed on 5 November 2026. The three games are the best-selling builders closest to Eco Town (`research/eco-town-market-comparables.md`, Tier A):

| Game | Sales | Footage reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| Cities: Skylines (Colossal Order / Paradox, 2015) | 12M+ copies | "Trailer 2020" (1:42), 10 store screenshots |
| Planet Coaster (Frontier, 2016) | 7.2M copies | Launch trailer (1:54), Gameplay Trailer 2 (0:48), 17 store screenshots |
| Planet Zoo (Frontier, 2019) | 5.4–5.5M copies | Launch trailer (2:23), 26 store screenshots |

The same method was then applied to our own `games/trailers/out/ecotown.mp4` (1:35) on the trailer branch, so the comparison is like for like.

## Method

- **Sources.** Official trailers and screenshots came from each game's Steam store page (appdetails API: app 255710, 493340 and 703080). Trailers were downloaded at 1920×1080.
- **Frames.** One frame was taken every 2 seconds (every 4 seconds for Eco Town) and tiled into time-stamped contact sheets. Every sheet was viewed. Interface frames were also pulled at full resolution and read closely: at 0:46, 0:48 and 0:56 in Cities: Skylines; 0:58, 1:00 and 1:04 in Planet Coaster; and 0:44 and 1:28–1:46 in Planet Zoo. Screenshots were read as 3×3 sheets, with the two most interface-heavy Planet Zoo screenshots read at full size.
- **Not included.** Third-party images are not committed to the repository. This report cites timestamps instead. The frames are in the session scratchpad only.

**Limits.**
- These are marketing trailers and curated screenshots, not play sessions. They show each game at its best and hide most of the interface.
- I tried to get real gameplay footage (beginner walkthroughs on YouTube). Search worked, but this environment's network policy blocks YouTube's video servers.
- Nobody played the games for this review. Claims about onboarding and the minute-to-minute feel are therefore not made here, except where a screenshot shows the interface directly.

## 1. Cities: Skylines

### How the trailer is built (1:42)

| Time | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:35 | Cinematic only, with no interface. A drone fly-over of a finished city, cycling through day, golden hour, sunset and night. Press quotes are set over the top ("The Finest City Builder in Over a Decade", Escapist 100/100; PC Gamer 86; Destructoid 90). |
| 0:36 | One kinetic word: **BUILD**, in white on a blue block. |
| 0:38–0:48 | First gameplay: a small grid of suburban streets, then a dam, then zoning. Zoning is painted on as translucent blue cells either side of a road, with a bar of seven large zone icons. |
| 0:46 | First data overlay (water). The world turns pale. Pipes and coverage show in blue, with a legend panel at top left. |
| 0:50 | **MANAGE**. |
| 0:52–0:56 | Overlays: buildings in solid blue on white, then a leisure-accessibility map. In the leisure map, roofs run from red to teal. A red footprint for a building that can't be placed shows "Construction cost: 1000 · Space already occupied! · Building must be placed roadside!". |
| 1:00 | **MOD IT**, followed by the asset and map editors: terrain brushes, tree brushes, a properties panel. |
| 1:16–1:30 | Expansions montage: night-life neon, snow, a tornado and a meteor from the disasters pack, a blimp, ferries, a green-cities set. |
| 1:32–1:42 | End card, then "Cities built by" with community creators' names. |

About 10% of the trailer shows the game's interface. The first gameplay appears at 36 seconds.

### Visual language

- **Realism at scale.** Real-world materials, long views to mountains and sea, thousands of small cars. The appeal is scale: "my city is huge and real".
- **Time of day as the mood control.** The trailer goes through four lighting states in its first 20 seconds. Night lights (street lights, lit windows, traffic trails) are the money shot.
- **The overlay look is the game's best-known visual idea.** When an overlay is on, the world drops to a clay-white model and one variable is painted in strong colour: blue for water, red-to-teal for access, red/amber/green for traffic (store screenshot 10). It works because everything else is removed.
- **The interface is dense and small.**
  - A bottom bar of about 20 small category icons, plus a demand bar (green/blue/yellow) at bottom left.
  - Money, population and a happiness face at bottom right.
  - A notification bird ("Chirper") with a count, top centre.
  - It is built for adults on a desktop monitor with a mouse.
- **The error message is attached to the item being placed.** It is red, sits under the cursor and names the rule that was broken.

### For Eco Town

- **Adopt**
  - Eco Town's lenses should use the clay-world grammar. When a lens opens, fade the island to a pale model and paint one variable on top: flood blue, carbon grey-brown, walking minutes as numbered colour bands, nature green.
  - **What to change:** today's trailer puts a "FLOOD LENS" label over an otherwise normal-looking island, so the flaw is not immediately readable.
- **Adopt**
  - Feedback that names the rule, attached to what is being placed, for example "Homes here flood in winter".
  - The Cities: Skylines rule is given in adult terms. Eco Town's should be in child terms with an icon.
- **Adopt**
  - Show night as the payoff: windows lit by the town's own wind and battery. It fits Level 5 exactly.
- **Avoid**
  - The dense toolbar, small icons and the stack of status numbers. None of it would work for ages 8–12 on a tablet. For 13–17 it is still too much for a 45-minute session.
- **Avoid**
  - Disasters as spectacle (tornado, meteor). This goes against Eco Town's "gentle consequences" rule, especially for flooding, which may be a real experience for some children (LAB-GUIDE.md §9B).

## 2. Planet Coaster

### How the trailer is built (launch trailer, 1:54)

| Time | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | ESRB "Everyone" rating card. |
| 0:06–0:22 | **A character story on empty land.** One man walks into an empty green valley, ties a rope and sits on a barrel bench. The park mascot, a cartoon dinosaur, drops in beside him. |
| 0:22–0:26 | **The park grows around him in a time-lapse**: buildings, a Ferris wheel, then a coaster loop sweeping over the bench. This is "empty land to whole world" in four seconds. |
| 0:28–0:46 | On-ride point-of-view shots: a log flume, inversions, a neon night street, riders' faces mid-scream. |
| 0:48 | **CREATE**, set over a crowd walking towards the camera. |
| 0:50 | A cobbled path being laid in a smooth curve on bare grass. The act of building is the shot. |
| 0:52–0:56 | Themed areas in turn (Wild West, pirate), with crowds wearing mascot hats. |
| 0:58 | **MANAGE** over a pirate bay, then a management panel (guest summary, thought percentages, finances). |
| 1:00 | **"New Discovery!"** card: a green ribbon, confetti and the new ride ("Whirly Rig · Family Ride"). |
| 1:04 | **Scenario result card**: 1 of 3 stars, "Pirate Battle · Medium objectives complete", four stat tiles (Most popular attraction · Guests in park 900 · Park cash · Date completed), Continue button. |
| 1:06–1:08 | **SHARE**: the in-game workshop with an avatar, featured creators and a Steam Workshop item. |
| 1:14–1:26 | Mascots and crowds, a dark ride, night. |
| 1:28–1:42 | **Bookend**: the same man is back on the bench, now in a busy park at dusk, and a woman joins him. Then the end cards. |

About 9% of the trailer shows the interface. Gameplay Trailer 2 (0:48) repeats the CREATE / MANAGE / SHARE structure in 25 seconds, then runs a subscribe end-card loop.

### Visual language

- **Bright, saturated, cartoon-stylised people** set against realistic rides and terrain. Faces are big and expressive in close-ups (store screenshots 2, 3, 4, 9).
- **People are the scale and the emotion.** Crowds fill paths, queues snake, guests react.
- **Reward moments are designed as objects:** ribbon banners, confetti, three stars, chunky stat tiles. They are the same in every scenario, so players learn to look forward to them.
- **The interface is clean, light and rounded,** with blue and white panels. It is readable but text-heavy; the management panel is a spreadsheet.

### For Eco Town

- **Adopt**
  - The **bookend structure** for the Eco Town trailer and the game's opening and ending. Start with the followed family arriving on the empty island, the way the man walks into the empty valley. End with the same family at the evening festival.
  - DESIGN.md already has a followed family. This makes them the frame of the whole experience.
- **Adopt**
  - The **in-place time-lapse**: the island builds itself around a fixed camera between "Foundations set" moments. The trailer engine can already do this.
- **Adopt**
  - A **single, consistent reward card** at the end of each level, Eco Town's Act 7 "Celebrate". It should have:
    - stars for spotting the AI's mistake;
    - three or four big tiles (Mistake caught · Habit named · Families moved in · Town-flower change);
    - one Continue button.
  - Planet Coaster's card is the model. Its "medium objectives" style of star tier also fits the rule that stars reward checking, not looks.
- **Adopt**
  - **Unlock cards** ("New Discovery!") for each new lens: "New lens: Flood lens" with confetti at the start of Level 1, and so on. This turns the "one new system per level" pattern into a moment the players notice.
- **Adopt**
  - Show building as an action. A path drawing itself, a hedge growing tile by tile, a roof lifting into place. These gestures should be satisfying on their own.
- **Avoid**
  - The spreadsheet management panel, for both age bands.
  - For 13–17, one compact "town report" panel is enough. The town flower plus the evidence log should do this job.
- **Avoid**
  - Thrill-ride point-of-view shots and anything that moves the camera fast. They conflict with the Reduce motion and no-camera-shake rules in DESIGN.md.

## 3. Planet Zoo

### How the trailer is built (launch trailer, 2:23)

| Time | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | ESRB "Everyone 10+" rating card (crude humour, mild blood, mild violence). |
| 0:04–0:30 | **A nature documentary, with no interface and no visitors.** Backlit savanna, a crocodile gliding through reeds, a giraffe silhouetted against the sunset, bison in an autumn snowfall, gorillas in jungle mist, flamingos at a lily pond, pandas in snow. Each biome gets its own light. |
| 0:32–0:42 | The zoo appears: visitors on paths, red pandas, giraffes beside a monorail, a safari jeep, a glasshouse station with a steam train, climbing frames. |
| 0:44 | **Empty, sunlit green hills**, then at 0:46 **"LET YOUR CREATIVITY RUN WILD"** over a finished themed area. A before-and-after cut. |
| 0:48–1:12 | Themed biomes: a Chinese temple with pandas, an Indian palace with tigers, a chimp climbing frame, a desert overview, a snow village, a night street, a formal garden, peacocks. |
| 1:14 | **"MAKE YOUR ZOOS THRIVE"**. |
| 1:16–1:26 | **Management shown as people doing jobs:** a keeper striding past a school group, a keeper raking an enclosure, a food-prep kitchen, a vendor waving from a kiosk. |
| 1:28–1:32 | **Overlays.** The world turns into a pale clay model. One shows temperature in orange with cool blue pools. One shows a white world with green dots. One shows a red patch marking an invalid habitat area, with a placement gizmo. |
| 1:34–1:36 | **Consequence shown in the world:** visitors marching with protest placards. |
| 1:38 | A vet treating an animal on an operating table. |
| 1:46 | **Learning inside the world:** a family at in-zoo information boards with the species name, conservation status and range map. |
| 1:48–2:10 | Close-ups of visitors' delight and animals roaring and playing. **"BUILD A WORLD FOR WILDLIFE"** at 1:56. A lion on a ridge at 2:08. |
| 2:12–2:23 | Logo and end card. |

There are **no interface panels at all** in the trailer. The only interface-like shots are three overlay frames (about 4%). The first visitor appears at 32 seconds.

### Visual language

- **Each biome has its own palette and light**: gold for savanna, green mist for jungle, blue-white for taiga and snow, rose for blossom. The documentary opening sells the *subject* (living animals) before the *game*.
- **The overlays use the same clay-world grammar as Cities: Skylines,** with a gradient legend ("Temperature", blue to red) in a small panel at the top (store screenshot 9).
- **Interface in the screenshots** (store screenshots 4 and 17, read at full size):
  - An **Objectives** panel with a story intro, a "Bronze" tier and a ticked checklist ("Locate the Grizzly Bear habitat ✓").
  - A **character advisor** with a portrait and a speech bubble ("Nancy Jones: …there can be as many as 40 lions in a pride…").
  - A **guest panel** with "Recent thoughts". Each thought has a smiley, one sentence and a locate pin, for example "That was a thirst quencher from Pipshot Juice 476", or a frowning face with "Maybe I'll come back to ATM 582 when it's less busy".
  - **Need bars** with icons.
  - A **Zoo overview** panel with star ratings for Animal, Conservation, *Guest Education*, Marketing and Happiness, plus an "Inspection report" envelope.
- **Habitat painting**: a circular brush and a small swatch palette (grass, sand, soil, rock) in a floating panel at bottom right.

### For Eco Town

- **Adopt**
  - The **subject-first opening**. Before any interface, show the island living: water moving, the meadow flowering, deer at the wood edge, bees, otters. This is the "before" that the AI's mistakes put at risk.
  - It makes the Nature petal something you feel, and it follows the evidence that restoring nature works as the win itself (Terra Nil, Timberborn).
- **Adopt**
  - **Same-angle before/after** at each level's end: an empty slope, then the built street, from one fixed camera.
- **Adopt**
  - **Management as people, not panels.** Show the Resident Voice as residents doing things: a parent cycling a cargo bike to school, the repair-café volunteer, a farmer at the hedge. This is already the spirit of DESIGN.md's living town, and Planet Zoo shows it reads in seconds.
- **Adopt, carefully, by age band**
  - **Consequence shown in the world** (the protest).
  - **8–12:** keep to DESIGN.md's gentle set: queues, shivering, a family moving out with boxes.
  - **13–17:** residents can bring a **petition to the town hall** on a bad decision, such as the drop-off car park in Level 2. It is a civic, non-violent reaction and gives the older version a real dilemma (section 4 of the comparables report).
- **Adopt**
  - **Recent thoughts with a locate pin.** Planet Zoo's guest-thought rows are exactly Eco Town's bubble system in list form. In the 13–17 version, add a "What residents said" list where each line has a locate pin that flies the camera to that resident. It is also the evidence for the Level 6 sampling-bias check: count the thoughts, and notice whose are missing.
- **Adopt**
  - **Learning objects in the world.** Planet Zoo's species boards teach without a lesson screen. Eco Town can put small "fact boards" in the street view: why the timber home is warm, how a rain garden works. Each board needs a sourced fact, per LAB-GUIDE.md §11.
- **Adopt**
  - **A tiered objectives checklist** (Bronze / Silver / Gold with ticks), adapted to Eco Town's three stars: ★ opened the right lens, ★★ named the habit, ★★★ chose the clearer card.
- **Avoid**
  - **Photoreal fidelity as the goal.** Eco Town runs on school Chromebooks in a browser. It should compete on readability and charm, Townscaper-style, not on fur shading.
  - The voxel style is a strength here. The trailer engine's flat-colour voxels with baked ambient occlusion already read clearly at small sizes.

**What the current Eco Town trailer does well that the benchmarks don't:**
- It explains its whole loop in 95 seconds, with no narration: the AI's guess with "% sure", the reveal, the clearer card, "Plan checked". Every benchmark trailer sells mood and leaves out the rules.
- For a school or partner audience that has to understand the lab, that is the right priority.
- The reveal lines read immediately at 1080p: "The meadow floods every winter", "8:45 am: children can't cross", "Still winter night: blackout".

**Where it falls behind:**
- It is an interface demo, not a world.
- The island never fills the frame.
- No resident is seen close enough to care about.
- The lenses don't change how the world looks.
- The "XP" counter adds clutter and isn't part of the scoring in DESIGN.md.

## 5. What to change

### For the game build (both versions unless marked)

1. **Lens look.**
   - When a lens opens, the island fades to a pale model over about 0.4 s and shows one variable in strong colour, with a small gradient legend in the top-left corner.
   - Colours must have pattern or number backups (waves, dots, minute numbers) for colour-blind players, per LAB-GUIDE.md §10.
2. **Interface budget.**
   - In the Live and Celebrate acts, at most two panels on screen; at most four during Plan, Check and Fix.
   - The helper card and the cards row should slide away when not needed.
   - The town flower collapses to a small icon until it changes.
3. **Drop the XP counter.** Stars only, earned for checking. This matches the shared rules in `games/PLAN.md`.
4. **Fill the frame with the world.**
   - The default sky camera frames the island at 70–80% of screen width.
   - Interface panels go over sky and sea, not over land.
5. **One end-of-level reward card**, Planet Coaster style: stars, a tile for each item listed in section 2, and Continue.
   - Add an **unlock card** at the start of each level for the new lens.
6. **A same-angle before/after** at "Foundations set": a short time-lapse of the level's builds appearing from one fixed camera.
7. **Residents up close.**
   - The followed family gets a close camera in street and inside views.
   - Faces and gestures should be readable at tablet size: waving, shivering, cheering.
   - Give the main characters larger, more expressive voxel faces than background residents.
8. **Consequences shown in the world, by age band.**
   - 8–12: queues, shivers, boxes.
   - 13–17: those, plus a residents' petition at the town hall, with a choice of how to respond.
9. **"What residents said" list with locate pins** (13–17). It feeds the Level 6 sampling-bias check.
10. **Fact boards in street view.** One per level, each with a cited source.
11. **A living-island opening**, around 10 seconds and skippable: water, the meadow, deer, bees. The family arrives by ferry, before the first brief.

### For the next trailer cut

- Open with 8–10 seconds of the living empty island, with no interface.
- Bookend with the followed family: arriving on the ferry at the start, at the festival at the end.
- Cut the interface down to the reveal moments. Keep the helper card and the one red reveal line; drop the palette, flower and switcher from most shots.
- Show at least two lenses in the clay-world style.
- Add a same-angle before/after for at least one level.
