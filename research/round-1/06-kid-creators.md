# Kids-as-creators tools: what worked, what stalled (research for DeltaOps)

Sources were found by web search on 2026-11-04. Figures marked **[est.]** are estimates or come from third parties. Some precise numbers (Labo totals, Game Builder Garage sales, current Scratch totals) could not be verified, and I say so below.

## 1. Product snapshots

**Bloxels (Pixel Press, later with Mattel)**
- **What it is.** A physical 13×13 board with coloured cubes. The child photographs the board and it becomes a playable platformer level, character or art. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel_Press
- **History.** It was funded on Kickstarter in 2015: $68k raised against a $40k goal. Mattel then handled manufacturing and distribution, and there was a Star Wars edition. https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/bloxels-the-video-game-you-can-build-launches-on-kickstarter-504241201.html , https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/the-building-blocks-of-game-design/
- **Recognition.** Best of Toy Fair 2015. Finalist for 2017 TIA Toy of the Year in the Game category. https://www.gamesandlearning.org/?p=3028 , https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/2017-toy-of-the-year-toty-award-finalists-unveiled-by-toy-industry-association-300366551.html
- **Colour code.** This is the core scaffold. Each of 8 colours means one rule:
  - Green = terrain
  - Red = hazard
  - Yellow = collectible or key
  - White = story, checkpoint or warp
  - Purple = enemy
  - Orange = action/throwable
  - Pink = power-up
  - Blue = liquid

  Source: https://bloxels.com/tutorials/8-block-types
- **Classroom use.** Bloxels EDU (2018) added a teacher hub that controls sharing and the arcade, plus per-student licences. https://www.projectpixelpress.com/blog-list/2018/7/12/introducing-bloxels-edu
- **Later history.** The old apps (Builder, Star Wars) can no longer be downloaded for the first time. Retail classroom hardware packs were marked discontinued in July 2024. The EDU app is still listed (Play Store update in October 2025). https://www.cdw.com/product/teq-bloxels-classroom-5-pack/5572486 , https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/bloxels-edu
- **Pitfalls.** Reviewers found camera capture "super finicky", and the games came out "too similar". Engagement starts high but "can ultimately lead to frustration". https://www.techlearning.com/resources/bloxels-build-play-share-your-own-video-games
- **Lesson.** The physical-to-digital bridge was the magic and also the weakest link. Its value is the board plus the colour grammar, not the scanning technology.

**Super Mario Maker 1 and 2 (Nintendo)**
- **Success.** Mario Maker 1 sold about 4.01M copies. Mario Maker 2 sold 3.93M in its first quarter, had 2M courses within 2 weeks and passed 10M courses. https://www.kitguru.net/tech-news/mustafa-mahmoud/super-mario-maker-2-reaches-new-milestone-with-over-10-million-courses-created/ , https://shacknews.com/article/112798/super-mario-maker-2-two-million-courses-uploaded
- **Scaffolding.**
  - A familiar, fixed grammar: you already know what a Goomba does.
  - The make → playtest loop is instant (one button).
  - Mario Maker 2 adds a story mode that models good levels.
- **Sharing.** Course World, with a rule that you must clear your own course before uploading it.
- **Decline lesson.** Wii U online services ended in April 2024, so courses that were never downloaded became unreachable. https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/53854
- **Takeaway.** Creations that depend on the cloud are fragile. Give children an artefact they can keep.

**Nintendo Labo and Game Builder Garage**
- **Labo.** Cardboard "Toy-Cons" plus a hidden "Garage" for programming.
  - The Variety Kit passed 1M copies, but sales dropped sharply after launch: about 120k in launch week in Japan, then about 2k a week **[est.]**. Nintendo admitted it "isn't selling gangbusters". https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2019/04/nintendo_labo_variety_kit_surpasses_one_million_sales , https://gamerant.com/nintendo-labo-disappearance-failure-history/
  - Lesson: the making was loved once, but the toy's appeal fades (and the cardboard wears out) and there was little reason to return.
- **Game Builder Garage (2021).** Seven heavily guided lessons with the characters Bob and Alice, plus quizzes, before free building.
  - Praised as accessible.
  - Criticised for being prescriptive, and the lessons can't be skipped.
  - https://www.shacknews.com/article/125217/game-builder-garage-review-build-your-own-adventure , https://nintendolife.com/reviews/nintendo-switch/game_builder_garage
  - No sales figure found.

**LittleBigPlanet (Media Molecule / Sony)**
- **Success.** About 4M copies of the first game, under the tagline "Play. Create. Share." Over 1M levels by July 2009 and more than 10M on the PS3 servers. https://blog.eu.playstation.com/2009/07/22/littlebigplanet-community-reaches-one-million-creations/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LittleBigPlanet
- **Moderation.**
  - Early press joked about how many levels were "phallus free". https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/13340/
  - In 2021 the servers went down after DDoS attacks and offensive messages, and in April 2024 the PS4 servers closed permanently. A decade of user-made content was partly lost. https://www.engadget.com/sony-littlebigplanet-ps3-vita-servers-shut-down-163356541.html , https://videogameschronicle.com/news/sony-confirms-littlebigplanet-3-shutdown-months-after-it-was-temporarily-taken-offline

**Dreams (Media Molecule, PS4)**
- An extraordinarily powerful tool with a steep learning curve.
- Live support ended in September 2023 and the curation team was later cut. Sales were described as lower than LittleBigPlanet's; no official figure **[est.]**. https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/media-molecule-is-ending-support-for-dreams-in-september/
- **Lesson.** A high ceiling without a low floor stays a niche.

**Kodu Game Lab (Microsoft Research / FUSE Labs)**
- 3D worlds programmed with "when–do" tiles on Xbox and PC, used in some schools.
- It is now maintained by a third party. https://kodugamelab.com/faq , https://www.eweek.com/development/microsoft-kicks-off-kodu-kids-programming-game-design-contest/
- **Lesson.** Strong pedagogy fades when its corporate sponsor moves on.

**Scratch (MIT)**
- **Scale.** About 121M registered users and more than 120M shared projects by 2023 **[est., third-party]**. Live totals: https://scratch.mit.edu/statistics/ , https://jetlearn.com/blog/scratch-statistics
- **Design principles.** Resnick's "low floors, high ceilings, wide walls", with remixing built into the platform. Hill and Shaw found causal evidence that wide walls raise engagement and learning. https://scratchfoundation.org/learn/learning-library/scratch-creative-learning-philosophy , https://blog.communitydata.science/testing-the-wide-walls-design-principle-in-the-wild/

**Roblox Studio**
- About 40M daily users under 13 in Q2 2025 **[est., Statista]**. https://www.statista.com/statistics/1190309/daily-active-users-worldwide-roblox
- **Safety.** Persistent child-safety problems led to restrictions in 2024: under-13s lost direct messaging and social hangouts became 13+. https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Child_safety_on_Roblox
- **Lessons.**
  - Studio is effectively a professional tool, so there is no low floor for 7–10-year-olds.
  - Open social sharing is the main source of risk.

**Minecraft Education**
- More than 40,000 school systems in 140 countries (vendor claim). https://education.minecraft.net/en-us
- **Themed build challenges.**
  - 2020 Global Build Challenge: "healthy spaces for animals and humans to coexist".
  - 2021 Global Build Challenge with UNESCO: "Peace with Nature".
  - Norway challenge (2020): about 2,000 students.
  - https://education.minecraft.net/blog/students-in-norway-take-up-the-2020-minecraft-education-challenge
- **Lesson.** A tight real-world brief, framed as a design problem, beats an open sandbox in a lesson. It is also directly relevant to nature centres.

**Drawn to Life (2007) and Draw a Stickman: EPIC**
- **Drawn to Life.** You draw your own hero and items, which are then dropped into a structured game. Several hundred thousand early sales, about 820k **[est., fan wiki]**. https://www.engadget.com/2011/02/10/5th-cell-reveals-sales-for-scribblenauts-drawn-to-life/
- **Draw a Stickman.** About 665k downloads in its first month. https://hitcents.com/news/draw-stickman-epic-has-huge-success-app-stores
- **Lesson.** "Your drawing becomes the character" is a strong hook, and the game around it supplies the structure.

**Toca Boca**
- 100M downloads by 2015 and more than 170M by 2017. https://tocaboca.com/press-post/toca-boca-celebrates-100-million-downloads/ , https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/10/kids-app-maker-toca-boca-debuts-its-first-consumer-product-collection-at-target/
- **Design.** Toy-like and open-ended. No winning, no text, no ads or in-app purchases, and short sessions that are complete in themselves. https://www.killscreen.com/secret-smart-kids-entertainment-give-them-toy-not-game/

**LEGO Builder's Journey (and LEGO Worlds)**
- **Builder's Journey.** A wordless voxel-diorama puzzler: Golden Joystick Mobile Game of the Year 2020 and 10M players, about 7M of them via an Epic Games Store giveaway. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego_Builder%27s_Journey , https://www.brickfanatics.com/lego-builders-journey-mega-milestone-giveaway
- **Lesson.** Small floating dioramas with constrained brick placement read beautifully, and that is close to DeltaOps' visual style.
- **LEGO Worlds.** No reliable figures found.

**Pixel art tools in schools**
- No authoritative usage figures found. Bloxels EDU explicitly markets pixel art and storytelling, and graph-paper or Minecraft pixel art is a common unplugged activity **[unverified, anecdotal]**.

## 2. Patterns that work

1. **A fixed, learnable grammar.** Bloxels colours, Mario parts and Kodu tiles all give a small vocabulary with obvious meanings, which beats a blank canvas.
2. **An instant make → play loop.** Mario Maker's one-button playtest and Bloxels' snap-and-play let children see consequences within seconds.
3. **"My drawing is in the game."** Drawn to Life and Bloxels show that personal ownership drives motivation.
4. **A themed brief instead of an empty world.** Minecraft build challenges and Game Builder Garage lessons give children a purpose to build towards.
5. **Low floor, wide walls.** Scratch shows that many valid answers to one brief sustain engagement.

## 3. Pitfalls

- **Blank-canvas paralysis.** Dreams, Roblox Studio and Labo's Garage have powerful tools that young children rarely reach unaided.
- **Same-y outputs.** Bloxels games were criticised as "too similar", so a constraint grammar needs real variety in what can be built.
- **Fragile capture technology.** Bloxels' camera scanning was the most common complaint.
- **Over-scripted tutorials.** Game Builder Garage's lessons could not be skipped and felt prescriptive.
- **Novelty decay.** Labo was loved for its build, but play dropped off quickly.
- **Moderating user-made content and keeping online services running.** LittleBigPlanet suffered offensive content and hacking, Roblox has ongoing child-safety problems, and the Mario Maker and LittleBigPlanet shutdowns erased creations. DeltaOps' no-accounts, no-sharing model avoids all of this. The finished artefact should be the "share".

## 4. Implications for DeltaOps

**Concept: "Build-a-Habitat Grid" (a Bloxels-style stage or mode)**
- **Physical side.** Each group gets a printed 8×8 grid mat and paper or foam colour tiles (or crayons). Use a Bloxels-like colour key on a printed legend card. For example:
  - Green = trees/cover
  - Blue = water
  - Brown = burrow/shelter
  - Yellow = food plants
  - Grey = path/people
  - Red = danger (road, predator)
- **Bridging to the screen without a camera.** The facilitator, or a child, taps the same pattern into an on-screen 8×8 grid on the island (tap to cycle colour), copying from the mat. This avoids Bloxels' capture problems while keeping the physical-first step.
- **The scripted "AI".** It classifies the design ("Hedgehog habitat — 72% sure") and the voxel island rebuilds from the grid, with blocky animals moving in or leaving.
- **Planned misreads.**
  - A vague instruction card ("make it safe") leads to ambiguous designs.
  - A grid with lots of blue gets labelled "pond, not meadow, 58% sure".
  - A grey path cutting through green is read as "two separate habitats".
  - Pre-scripted rules: colour ratio, adjacency and connectedness map to label + confidence.
- **Retry.** Children change 3 tiles and watch the label and confidence shift.

**Facilitator questions**
- "What did the AI 'see' that made it say pond? Which tiles convinced it?"
- "Why only 58% sure? What would make it more sure?"
- "Is the AI wrong, or was our instruction card unclear?"
- "Would a real hedgehog agree with the AI?"
- "If we trained it only on ponds, what would it think of everything?"

**End artefact.** A printed "Habitat Blueprint" card showing the child's grid, the AI's final label and % sure, and one line circled by the child: "The AI got it right / wrong because…". It is taken home, with no upload. The Mario Maker and LittleBigPlanet shutdowns are the cautionary examples here.

**Recommendations**
1. **Use a fixed colour grammar of 6–8 meanings on one printed legend card.** Bloxels' 8 block types are the model. Keep colours consistent across all labs.
2. **Go physical first, but don't depend on a camera.** Mat → tap-copy (or the facilitator enters it) avoids Bloxels' "finicky" capture.
3. **Never present a blank canvas.** Each stage starts from a part-built island with a themed brief, like the Minecraft Global Build Challenges. Avoid the Dreams and Roblox Studio pattern.
4. **Make the loop take seconds.** Grid change → AI guess → world change, ideally in under 10 seconds, like Mario Maker's instant playtest.
5. **Design the misreads deliberately.** Script 2–3 known ambiguous patterns per stage so the vague card reliably produces a teachable error.
6. **Widen the walls inside a narrow brief.** Several valid habitats (hedgehog, frog, bee) avoid Bloxels' "games too similar" problem (Scratch's wide walls).
7. **Keep guidance light and skippable.** One short model example, then hand over control. Game Builder Garage's long tutorials are the warning.
8. **No sharing infrastructure.** No accounts, no gallery and no moderation burden. The printed artefact is the share, which avoids the LittleBigPlanet and Roblox risks.
9. **Toca-style sessions.** Each stage takes about 5–8 minutes, is wordless and has no "losing", so the AI being "wrong" is the interesting part, not a failure.
10. **Visual polish matters.** Builder's Journey shows that small voxel dioramas win awards. Make the world's reaction to each grid change visible and satisfying, such as animals arriving.
