## Changes from round 2

**Corrections to round-2 claims (these change how the evidence should be quoted):**

1. **Ke & Grabowski (2007) compared different conditions from those round 2 gave.** The conditions were cooperative Teams-Games-Tournament play, competitive play and **no game (drills)**, not "individual play". Round 2 said learning gains were "similar across conditions". In fact game play beat drills on maths performance, and cooperative play was best for attitudes. https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8535.2006.00593.x
2. **Plass et al. (2013): N = 58, and collaboration had a cost.** Collaboration *lowered* performance during the play session (more restarts). Competition raised in-game learning. Out-of-game fluency did not differ by condition. Collaboration was the only mode that raised the intention to play again. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032688
3. **Brain Points (O'Rourke et al. 2014) effects are real but small:** r = 0.07, with a median of 118 s vs 89 s of play. The "lower performers" result means more struggling players were still playing at 30 minutes; it is not a bigger gain for them. http://eleanorourke.com/papers/brainpoints_chi.pdf
4. **Lomas et al. (2013) has a caveat that round 2 left out.** The most engaging (easiest) conditions "produced the slowest rates of learning". Also, the samples (about 10K and 70K) are *game sessions*, not unique children. https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/pubs/Lomas,%20Forlizzi%20&%20Koedinger%202013.pdf
5. **Christy & Fox (2014), direction now confirmed.** Women who saw a *female*-majority leaderboard scored *lower* than those who saw a male-majority one, though their academic identification was higher. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131514001195
6. **Sailer & Homner (2020) is weaker than round 2 implied.** Only the cognitive effect held up in high-rigour studies; the motivational and behavioural effects were "less stable". The competition-plus-collaboration advantage applies to behavioural outcomes. https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w.pdf
7. **Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006) used no adolescent sample.** It comprised three undergraduate studies plus 730 MMO players aged 16–44. https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf
8. **The Tsunku "Just make it simple" quote is from *Iwata Asks in Writing: Rhythm Heaven Megamix* (2016),** not the DS Iwata Asks. The same answer continues: "Don't make a hard game." http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/feature/43066/iwata-asks-in-commemoration-part-3-rhythm-heaven-megamix-iwata-asks-in-writing-rhythm-heaven-megamix
9. **Duolingo ABC is for ages 3–6** (App Store), not 3–8. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-to-read-duolingo-abc/id1440502568
10. **Meta Quest change, narrowed.** In 2023 Meta introduced *parent-managed accounts for ages 10–12*; some apps stayed 13+. https://www.meta.com/blog/meta-accounts-parent-managed-families/
11. **Fall Guys: the "about 2-minute rounds" figure is unsupported.** A whole show usually lasts "not longer than 10 minutes" (Polygon). The game went free-to-play with a Season Pass in 2022. https://www.polygon.com/2020/8/20/21375212/fall-guys-game-design-randomness-esports-success-sales ; https://www.fallguys.com/news/fall-guys-is-now-free-for-all?lang=en-US
12. **Crossy Road is not free of randomness.** Coins buy random character "pulls" from a prize machine. Round 2 listed it only as a fair-ad model. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-i-crossy-road-i-made-1-million-from-video-ads
13. **Flappy Bird's follow-up was *Flappy Birds Family*** (Amazon Fire TV, multiplayer, pitched as "less addictive"). https://techcrunch.com/2014/08/01/flappy-bird-returns-as-a-multiplayer-game-but-only-for-amazons-fire-tv/
14. **The RobTop Q&A URL is replaced.** The paywalled fandom link gives way to the Official Geometry Dash Wiki transcript of the 27 March 2015 Q&A, now read in full. https://geometrydash.wiki.gg/wiki/RobTop_Q%26A_March_2015

## 1. Primary designer sources

### 1.1 RobTop / Geometry Dash

**What Robert Topala has said (all [V]):**

*Cult of Mac*, 27 June 2014 (interview) https://www.cultofmac.com/news/geometry-dash-robert-topala
- On the origin: "There really was no detailed plan… It simply started as a template with a cube that could crash and jump. Through different iterations I then kept adding more and more features and content until the game felt right."
- On inspiration: the article says he was inspired by Super Mario Bros.-style platformers and by "The Impossible Game, BIT.TRIP RUN! and Super Meat Boy". Super Meat Boy is the game whose designer explicitly argued for instant respawn (§1.3).
- On user content: "The amount of user-generated content is insane." At that point there were more than 500,000 custom stages and about 20 million downloads.
- On the editor: "I often see things I didn't even think was possible to do in the editor."

*Game Developer* guest post, 11 Feb 2015 https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/prioritize-players-and-their-experience-to-succeed-in-gaming
- "Every game designer walks a fine line. On one hand, you want to create something simple that anyone can pick up and play. On the other hand, if the game is too simple, then your most loyal players will get bored quickly and leave."
- "I create the tools so that users are empowered to do whatever they want with them… it's honestly as much their game as it is mine."
- "Any new feature I add will be applied in a million different ways that I hadn't even thought of by my users."

Geometry Dash Wiki Q&A, 27 March 2015 (his answers transcribed by the wiki's organiser) https://geometrydash.wiki.gg/wiki/RobTop_Q%26A_March_2015
- On the origin: "I really enjoy platform games, and I wanted to put some ideas into rhythm platformers. I built the base and then kept adding things to make it fun."
- On level design: "Build a level that has good structure, and is synchronized (more/less) with the music… Don't add too much, sometimes a simple design will give a better result."
- On difficulty: "It's hard even for me. I am better at official levels since you know the timing of your own levels better."
- Favourite part: "User-created levels and the community."
- *Agent-reported, not seen in our own fetch excerpt* [S]: on beating his own demons, "With lots of attempts :)".

*Geometry Dash 10-Year Anniversary* video, Aug 2023 (RobTopGames YouTube) [S, transcript via Exa agent]: thanks the "creative and passionate community". https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mWi_zuXBECw

**Gap still open:** no primary RobTop statement explains *why* restart is instant, why the attempt counter exists or why practice mode is designed as it is. Four Exa searches and one agent run found none. The design lineage is inferred [est.]: RobTop names Super Meat Boy as an inspiration, and McMillen's postmortem gives the instant-respawn rationale (§1.3).

**Restart and progress features (Official Geometry Dash Wiki) [V]:**
- **Auto-Retry** is "enabled by default. Automatically respawns the player after every crash." **"Enable Faster Reset: Restarts in 0.5 seconds instead of 1.0 second upon death."** So the default failure-to-retry gap is about 1 s. https://geometrydash.wiki.gg/wiki/Settings
- **Attempt counter.** Its existence is confirmed by the "Hide Attempts: Hides the attempt counter" setting (same page).
- **Progress displays.** The pause menu shows "Normal mode and practice mode first-time progress meters", which are best-progress bars. It also has optional on-screen "Show Progress Bar" and "Show Percentage". https://geometrydash.wiki.gg/wiki/Pause_Menu
- **Practice mode.** "Allows checkpoints to be set at will… Progress on practice mode is recorded separately from that of normal mode and does not offer the standard level rewards." https://geometrydash.wiki.gg/wiki/Practice_Mode

**Scale [V, from round 2, Wikipedia]:**
- More than 1.1 billion mobile downloads by June 2024.
- Steam peak of 88,346 after update 2.2.
- "Over 150 million" level IDs; treat this as an upper bound.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Dash ; https://www.pcgamesn.com/geometry-dash/2-2-steam

**Children as an audience [V, round 2]:** Common Sense Media lists 323 kid reviews against 68 parent reviews. https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/geometry-dash

**What this suggests [est.]:** Geometry Dash's replay engine has three layers:
1. a failure loop of about 1 second;
2. honest, visible best-progress numbers, kept separate from practice;
3. a creator ecosystem.

RobTop's own words stress the third layer and steady iteration.

## 3. Research on children aged about 7–13

### 3.1 What drives replay and persistence

- **Self-determination theory: Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006)** [V], *Motivation and Emotion* 30(4):347–363, doi:10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8.
  - Samples: three undergraduate studies (n = 89, 50, 58) and 730 MMO players aged 16–44.
  - Finding: autonomy, competence and relatedness "independently predict enjoyment and future game play."
  - **No child sample.**
  - https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf
- **SDT with 8–12-year-olds** [S, round 2]: choice is linked to autonomy and intrinsic motivation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3311069
- **Elective replay in ST Math: Liu, Cody, Barnes, Lynch & Rutherford (2017), EDM** [S, via Exa agent; abstract on the PDF's first page].
  - Sample: 4,827 pupils in grades 3–5; 2,889 of them replayed by choice.
  - Finding: "some elective replay patterns were associated with learning, whereas others indicated that students were struggling."
  - http://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2017/proc_files/papers/paper_32.pdf
- **Zhang & Rutherford (2022), LAK** [S, via Exa agent]. Grade 5, California, regression sample n = 312. Replay "might be beneficial… if they chose to replay previously passed games when failing at a higher, more difficult level… and if they passed the replayed games." The design is observational. https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3506860.3506873
- **Klein-Latucha & Hershkovitz (2024), *Journal of Learning Analytics*** [S, via Exa agent]. 44,323 Israeli pupils in grades 1–6, about 3.1M applet runs. Sequences of rerunning correlated with success. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2023.8219
- **A dedicated study of replay *counts* in a facilitated session for 7–13-year-olds was still not found.** The ST Math work is the closest, but it is self-paced school software.

### 3.2 Challenge, failure and effort rewards

- **Lomas, Patel, Forlizzi & Koedinger (2013), CHI, doi:10.1145/2470654.2470668** [V]
  - Setting: Battleship Numberline on BrainPOP (grades 4–8 site). 10,478 and 69,642 *sessions*.
  - Main finding: "subjects were more engaged and played longer when the game was easier." For example, the largest targets brought 34% more estimates and 18% more time than the smallest.
  - Caveat: "Troublingly… the most engaging design conditions produced the slowest rates of learning."
  - Implication [est.]: generous success is good for staying, but the *learning* moment must not be removed. In DeltaOps that moment is the vague card's mistake.
  - https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/pubs/Lomas,%20Forlizzi%20&%20Koedinger%202013.pdf
- **O'Rourke, Haimovitz, Ballweber, Dweck & Popović (2014), CHI, "Brain Points", doi:10.1145/2556288.2557157** [V]
  - Design: about 15,000 children played *Refraction*. Points were given for effort, strategy and incremental progress.
  - Time played: median 118 s vs 89 s (+33%; r = 0.07).
  - Levels: mean 6.7 vs 5.5.
  - Strategy use: 2.49 vs 2.18 triggers per minute.
  - Among players still on at 30 minutes, 49% were "struggling" vs 37% in control.
  - The authors call the effects "very small", and the challenge-level analyses were only trends.
  - http://eleanorourke.com/papers/brainpoints_chi.pdf
- **O'Rourke, Chen, Haimovitz, Dweck & Popović (2015), L@S, doi:10.1145/2724660.2728686** [S, via Exa agent]. n = 7,940. Brain points "increase student persistence and use of strategy" but "are less engaging for low-income students." http://eleanorourke.com/papers/mindset_demographics_las.pdf
- **O'Rourke, Peach, Dweck & Popović (2016), L@S, doi:10.1145/2876034.2876040** [V]. About 25,000 children across five versions.
  - "Growth mindset animations cause a large number of players to quit, while brain points encourage persistence."
  - "Awarding brain points randomly is ineffective; the incentive structure is successful specifically because it rewards desirable growth mindset behaviors."
  - A summary-screen **progress visualisation increased retention**: players without it played a median of 2.26 vs 2.54 min.
  - http://eleanorourke.com/papers/brainpoints_deeper_look_las.pdf
- **Mueller & Dweck (1998), *JPSP* 75(1):33–52** [V]. Across six studies, fifth graders praised for intelligence (vs effort) showed "less persistence, less enjoyment, more low-ability attributions, and worse performance" after failure. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9686450/
- **Cubillos, Roncagliolo & Cabrera-Paniagua (2023), *Education Sciences* 13(11):1119** [S, via Exa agent; open access].
  - Design: randomised; 73 pupils in grades 3–4, Chile.
  - Finding: three-try feedback "outperformed" single-try correct-answer feedback on learning gains, with higher perceived competence and autonomy, but more perceived pressure.
  - https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci13111119
- **Israel-Fishelson & Hershkovitz (2020), *JECR* 58(5)** [S, via Exa agent]. CodeMonkey data, n = 2,040, grades 1–6. Persistence was positively associated with difficulty. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0735633119887187
- **Juul** [V]. Players "clearly prefer feeling responsible for failing". The players who failed some and then completed rated games highest; those who never failed rated them lower. The sample was not children. https://www.jesperjuul.net/text/fearoffailing/ ; MIT Press, *The Art of Failure* (2013): https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262019057/the-art-of-failure/
- **Lomos et al. (2023), *Education Sciences* 13(7):668** [S, via Exa agent]. Eye-tracking with n = 8 children aged 8–11 in Luxembourg: "students paid minimal visual attention to the incentive system during the game." Implication [est.]: stars must be revealed *after* the action, in a dedicated moment, or children will not notice them. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci13070668

### 3.3 Attention within a session
- The "2–5 minutes per year of age" rule is widely repeated by clinics and blogs (e.g. https://www.niagarachildrenscentre.com/OpenFileNew.ashx?id=133). A review of the claims found "very little actual research cited" (eLearning Industry, https://elearningindustry.com/attention-span-crisis). No primary validation study was found [V that this gap exists; not proof that the rule is false].
- Verified short loops:
  - Geometry Dash restart is about 1 s [V].
  - WarioWare microgames last 3–5 s [V].
  - A Wordle game is a single daily puzzle [V].
  - A Fall Guys show is under 10 min [V].
  - The PvZ text cap is 8 words [V].
- **Working assumption [est.]:** 30–90-second action loops inside a 30–60-minute facilitated session, with a discussion pause every 2–3 stages. This matches the ICO's "taking breaks" and "mid-level pause and save" recommendations (§4.1).

### 3.4 Competition, collaboration and leaderboards

**With children:**
- **Ke & Grabowski (2007), *BJET* 38(2):249–259** [V]
  - 125 fifth graders, assigned to cooperative TGT, competitive play or no gameplay (drills).
  - "Gameplaying was more effective than drills in promoting maths performance, and cooperative gameplaying was most effective for promoting positive maths attitudes regardless of students' individual differences."
  - https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8535.2006.00593.x
- **Plass et al. (2013), *J. Educ. Psych.* 105(4):1050–1066** [V]
  - n = 58 urban middle-school students.
  - Compared with individual play: competition raised in-game learning, while collaboration lowered in-session performance.
  - Both social modes raised interest, enjoyment and mastery orientation.
  - Only collaboration raised the "intentions to play the game again".
  - Out-of-game fluency did not vary by condition.
  - https://paulokeefe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Plass-OKeefe-et-al-2013.pdf
- **Almo, Rocha, Brennan & Dondio (2024), *IJSG* 11(4)** [V]
  - 1,389 Irish primary pupils, a 6-week maths game with an **infinite leaderboard**.
  - Leaderboard enjoyment depended on rank and on maths anxiety: "Maths anxious players disliked the leaderboard more than non-anxious players, even after controlling for their position."
  - The design is correlational; it is not proof that leaderboards *cause* anxiety.
  - https://journal.seriousgamessociety.org/index.php/IJSG/article/view/794
- **Dondio et al. (2023), GALA**, n = 434, same programme [S]. https://researchprofiles.tudublin.ie/en/publications/not-only-amatter-ofposition-player-traits-which-influence-theexpe/

**With adults:**
- **Hanus & Fox (2015), *Computers & Education* 80:152–161** [V]
  - 71 undergraduates over 16 weeks. The gamified course (badges and a leaderboard bundled together) showed "less motivation, satisfaction, and empowerment over time" and lower exam scores.
  - A 2018 corrigendum exists; its scope was not checked.
  - https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019
- **Christy & Fox (2014), *Computers & Education* 78:66–77** [V]. 80 female undergraduates; see Changes, item 5. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131514001195
- **Kahoot:**
  - Adkins-Jablonsky et al. 2021 [V, round 2]: rated less anxiety-inducing; lower performers were more likely to say it reduced exam stress. https://lifescied.org/doi/10.1187/cbe.20-08-0187
  - Wang & Tahir 2020, a literature review of 93 studies [V]: mostly positive, "however, there are also studies where Kahoot! has little or no effect". Reported problems include time pressure and fear of losing. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103818
  - DiFrancesca & Spencer 2025 [S, via Kahoot blog]: no differences between high- and low-competitive students when sessions were low-stakes. https://kahoot.com/blog/2026/01/14/kahoot-impact-competition-education-research/
- **Sailer & Homner (2020) meta-analysis** [V]
  - Effects: cognitive g = .49, motivational .36, behavioural .25.
  - Only the cognitive effect was stable in high-rigour studies.
  - "Combining competition with collaboration" was "particularly effective… for fostering behavioral learning outcomes".
  - "Mere competition might be problematic… at least for some learners."
  - https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w.pdf

**Design lesson [est.]:**
- **Team-level** comparison only; no infinite or individual leaderboards.
- **Improvement-based** comparison against your own earlier run.
- **Several kinds of star**, so the same children don't lose every time.
- **Collaboration as the default**, since it best predicts wanting to play again (Plass). It may slow in-session performance, so do not time it.

## 6. Detailed designs

### 6.1 Stars for clarity (three stars per stage, three different kinds)

**Principles:**
- Each star rewards a different thing ("multiple ways to shine"; Sailer & Homner [V]).
- ★3 is awarded **only for a specific, observable behaviour**: random or blanket praise did nothing in Brain Points (O'Rourke 2016 [V]).
- Thresholds are set by playtest (Mäki [V]).

| Star | Name (child-facing) | Earned when | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★1 | **World changed** | The team completes the stage with any card | Always given. It is never withheld for the vague card. |
| ★2 | **Crystal clear** | The chosen card's scripted "% sure" is ≥ 80% *and* the outcome matches the card's intent | Exactly one card per stage meets this bar on its own. A vague card can earn it after a **"Fix it" retry** with an add-on tile ("the *red* one", "*three* of them"). This is the multiple-try pattern (Cubillos 2023) and it stays untimed. |
| ★3 | **Assumption spotter** | The team picks the right picture for "What did the AI think you meant?" (1 of 3), or the facilitator taps "great explanation" | Contingent on the behaviour, as Brain Points were. Earnable on a "wrong" route, which makes the vague card a good choice. |

**Changes from round 2:**
- **No growth-mindset animations or "your brain grows!" cut-scenes.** In Refraction they made "a large number of players" quit [V]. The message belongs in the facilitator card, spoken by an adult.
- **Reveal stars in their own moment after the world change.** Do not show them in a corner during play: children aged 8–11 barely looked at an in-play incentive system (Lomos 2023 [S]).
- **Keep the vague-card mistake.** Lomas found that the most engaging (easiest) settings taught slowest [V]. Generous stars are fine, but the learning beat (AI misreads, world changes, discussion) must happen on every first run.

**Calibration [est.]:**
- ★1 for about 100% of teams.
- ★2 on 50–65% of stages at first play.
- ★3 on 40–60%.
- A full 18/18 takes 2–3 runs.
- Re-tune after 10+ pilot sessions; Rovio uses 5–10 testers per level [V].

**UI:**
- Three star slots per stage on the island ring. The 18 slots form a "star tray".
- ★3 is a magnifying-glass star in a different colour.
- Empty slots show a hint icon with an 8-word hint (PvZ [V]), for example "Try saying *which* tree."
- Copy praises the strategy, never the child (Mueller & Dweck [V]): "You said *how many*! The AI was 92% sure."
- No timer, and no star for speed.
- End-of-run montage (SMB [V]).

**Team board:**
- Opt-in, and team-level only.
- Shows "stars gained since your first run" and the ★3 count.
- **No infinite ranking list**: anxious children disliked one regardless of rank (Almo 2024 [V]).

### 6.2 "Spot the AI's assumptions" sticker book

**Concept.**
- Each vague or partial card hides named assumptions.
- The book collects them: optional, never blocking, shown as silhouettes (Celeste strawberries [V], "only collect them if you really want to!" [V]; GD coins [V]).

**Inventory [est.]:** per stage, the vague card has 2 assumptions, the partial card 1 and the clear card 0, so **18 stickers per lab**.

**Eight families**, a reusable prompt-writing vocabulary:
1. Which one?
2. How many?
3. How big / how much?
4. Where?
5. When / what order?
6. What kind?
7. Who for?
8. Missing step

**Mechanics:**
- **Unlock.** A sticker unlocks when the team chose a card with that assumption *and* identified it. This is the same moment as ★3, so the reward stays behaviour-contingent [V rationale].
- **No random drops and no duplicates.** This contrasts with the Crossy Road pulls [V] and with the ICO's advice to risk-assess randomised rewards [S].
- **Family completion** gives a cosmetic voxel hat for the AI helper.
- **Facilitator prompt:** "Which family do you think AIs fall for most?"

**UI:**
- A 3 × 6 grid of voxel snapshots, each with a caption of 8 words or fewer: "AI guessed **big apple**, not **many apples**."
- Progress shows as "11 of 18 found". No percentage bar and no "complete to win".

### 6.3 The 729-route map

**Honest constraint [est.]:** a team plays 2–5 full routes per session, so 729 cannot be a personal to-do list.

**Layer A: Outcome grid (personal).**
- 18 outcome tiles (6 stages × 3 cards). Seen tiles fill in; unseen tiles are fog-covered islands.
- Completable in 2–4 runs. This follows the Bloons medal-grid model [V].
- **New evidence:** a summary-screen progress visualisation raised retention in Refraction (O'Rourke 2016 [V]). GD's best-progress meters do the same job [V].
- Optional 6–8 named **ending islands**, made by clustering routes (e.g. by the number of clear cards chosen).

**Layer B: Site constellation (collective).**
- A radial sunburst with rings of 3, 9, 27, 81, 243 and 729 segments.
- Routes walked by any team at this site stay faintly lit: "Our camp has explored **214 of 729**."
- This is a collective goal, matching the collaboration and replay-intention evidence (Plass [V]; Ke & Grabowski [V]).
- Unexplored early segments sparkle.
- **Data:**
  - stored on the facilitator device only, as a 729-bit bitmap plus counts;
  - no timestamps tied to individuals;
  - a "reset for new group" button;
  - an optional poster PNG export.

**Previous-team ghost.**
- Translucent figures walk to the card the previous team at this site chose.
- Anonymous by default.
- Prompt: "Last team picked this one. Would you?" (Trackmania/Toad Rally [S]).

### 6.4 New: "Go back and nail it"

**What.** After a stage where the team misses ★2, the island offers (neutral copy, one tap, optional): "Want to warm up on an earlier stage you nailed?"

**Why.** In ST Math, replay after failure was associated with better post-test scores when pupils chose to replay *previously passed, easier* content and *passed it again* (Zhang & Rutherford 2022 [S]). The design is observational, so treat this as a hypothesis to pilot [est.].

**Guardrails:**
- Never automatic (ICO Standard 5, "active choice" [V]).
- Never required.
- Capped at once per stage, so it cannot loop.

## 7. Take-home island code (resume without accounts or personal data)

**Goals:** no typing, no personal data, no server lookup, readable by a 7-year-old, and works at another venue running the same lab.

| Field | Bits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Format version | 2 | |
| Lab ID | 6 | Up to 64 labs |
| Stars (6 × 3) | 18 | ★2 and ★3 independent |
| Stickers (18) | 18 | |
| CRC-6 checksum | 6 | See below |
| **Total** | **50** | 50 ÷ 5 bits = **10 icons** |

**Encoding:**
- A palette of 32 picturable voxel icons (5 bits each): fox, acorn, boat, comet, drum and so on.
- Two rows of five on a pocket "Island Passport" card, with a QR code carrying the same bits plus extras: outcome tiles (18 bits), endings (8) and hat (3).
- Entry by tapping icons on a 32-icon grid, or by kiosk QR scan.

**Error detection (corrected from round 2).** Round 2 claimed CRC-6 "catches about 98%". The more useful facts are:
- (a) Any CRC whose generator has a non-zero constant term detects **every burst error no longer than its degree** (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_cyclic_redundancy_checks ; Williams College notes, https://www.cs.williams.edu/~tom/courses/336/outlines/lect7_2.html). **One wrong icon** changes at most 5 consecutive bits, so a CRC-6 **always detects a single mis-entered icon**.
- (b) For longer or multiple errors, such as two swapped icons, the chance of slipping through is about 1 in 64 (2⁻⁶), so about 98.4% are detected [est., standard arithmetic].
- (c) Round 2's row-level hint ("check row 2?") needs extra parity bits. Either move to 11 icons (55 bits, leaving 5 spare bits for row parity) or keep 10 icons with a generic hint ("One picture looks wrong; check them with a grown-up") [est.].

**Lite code:** drop the stickers, giving 2 + 6 + 18 + 4-bit check = 30 bits = 6 icons. A 4-bit check still catches every single wrong icon only if the error burst is ≤ 4 bits, so prefer CRC-5 (35 bits = 7 icons) [est.].

**Privacy rules** [est.; ICO spirit, §4.1]:
1. Game state only: no name, age, school, date, venue or device ID.
2. Decode client-side. Never log codes, and never log them alongside IP addresses or timestamps.
3. Codes are not unique; many children share the same state.
4. Codes are never used on leaderboards.
5. The card tells grown-ups: "This card saves your child's island progress. It contains no personal information."
6. Neutral copy: "Want to keep exploring next time? Bring your card!" Never "Your stars will be lost!" (ICO Standard 5, "neutrally" [V]).
7. No expiry and no streaks (contrast Duolingo's loss aversion [V]).

## 8. What NOT to copy

- **Streaks, loss-aversion prompts and daily-return pressure.** Duolingo says it uses loss aversion [V]; ICO Standard 5 asks for neutral continue options [V].
- **Lives, energy, and timers on thinking.** McMillen [V]; Kahoot's speed scoring can encourage guessing (Wang & Tahir [V]).
- **Random, paid or membership-gated rewards.** Crossy Road pulls [V]; Prodigy [V]; ICO games tips on randomised rewards [S].
- **Paid "skip stage".** Roblox pattern [V].
- **Infinite or individual leaderboards.** Almo 2024 [V]; Hanus & Fox [V].
- **Growth-mindset cut-scenes.** O'Rourke 2016 [V].
- **Random or blanket praise.** O'Rourke 2016 [V]; Mueller & Dweck [V].
- **Help-use markers.** Celeste removed its Assist save-file icon [V].
- **Luck-based catch-up that decouples winning from understanding.** Blooket Gold Quest stealing [V]; Mario Party Bonus Stars [V].
- **Endless content plus auto-continue.** Flappy Bird [V]; Wardle's endless 2013 prototype [V]; ICO on "auto-play features" [V].

## 9. Recommendations, ranked

1. **Ship stars for clarity (§6.1)** with three different star types, a behaviour-contingent ★3, generous thresholds and strategy-praise copy. *Mäki; O'Rourke 2014/2016; Mueller & Dweck.*
2. **Make the vague card a reward, not a trap**, while keeping its mistake on every first run. *Brain Points; Lomas (engagement vs learning).*
3. **Add the assumption sticker book (§6.2)**: deterministic, never blocking, no random drops. *Celeste; ICO on randomised rewards.*
4. **Use the outcome grid as the personal progress view and the 729 constellation as a site-wide goal (§6.3).** *O'Rourke 2016 progress visualisation; Bloons; Plass/Ke & Grabowski.*
5. **Make retry about 1 s and unpunished, with an untimed "Fix it" second try.** *GD settings; SMB; Cubillos 2023.*
6. **Keep growth-mindset messages with the facilitator, not on screen.** *O'Rourke 2016.*
7. **Show an end-of-run montage**, revealed in its own moment. *SMB; Lomos 2023.*
8. **Team-only, opt-in, improvement-based boards; never an infinite ranking.** *Almo 2024; Sailer & Homner; Hanus & Fox.*
9. **Use the island-code passport (§7) as the persistence layer**, with CRC-6 and no personal data. *ICO Standard 5.*
10. **Pilot "go back and nail it" (§6.4)**, an optional Beat Mode warm-up, a facilitator break every 2–3 stages and a neutral finish screen. *Zhang & Rutherford; Tsunku; ICO age-band table.*
