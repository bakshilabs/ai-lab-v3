# Eco Town: best-selling world-building games and what to take from them

Research date: 5 November 2026. This is for the Eco Town game, which comes in two versions: ages 8–12 and ages 13–17. See `games/ecotown/DESIGN.md`.

The question: which commercially successful games are closest to Eco Town (building a whole town or world, residents who live in it, consequences you can see)? What made them sell, and which of those reasons fit a classroom game with no purchases?

## How to read this report

Every figure is labelled with one of three tags:
- **Official**: from the developer or publisher, or from a financial report.
- **Estimate**: from a third-party tracker such as SteamSpy, Gamalytic, VG Insights or Raijin. These are modelled numbers, often wrong by ±50%.
- **Players**: players, visits or audience. These are not copies sold and include free or subscription players.

The three are not comparable, so they are not mixed in one ranking. Age ratings are given only where they are well established. The rest are marked *to verify* and need checking against PEGI/ESRB before anything is printed.

A first draft of this report was produced by a research agent and then reviewed claim by claim. The corrections are listed at the end.

## 2. What the hits have in common, and what it means for Eco Town

Each pattern below is tied to specific games and to a specific Eco Town decision.

1. **The world reacts before the score does.** Townscaper, Dorfromantik and Animal Crossing reward each placement straight away with animation, sound and small creatures appearing. Score comes second.
   **Eco Town:** every build pops in with a sound and a small reaction, such as birds landing, a light coming on or a resident waving. The town flower updates afterwards.
2. **Overlays are how players understand the town.** Cities: Skylines' traffic, noise and land-value views are its best-known feature. Eco Town's lenses do the same job, with one extra step: the AI helper is wrong about what the lens will show.
   - **8–12:** one lens at a time, using icons and patterns as well as colour.
   - **13–17:** lenses can be combined, with real units (tonnes CO₂e, kWh, minutes walking) and a trade-off chart.
3. **Residents who say what they need.** RollerCoaster Tycoon's guest thoughts, Anno's needs tiers and Stardew Valley's villager routines make the simulation readable as people, not numbers. This matches DESIGN.md's bubbles and its followed family.
   **Eco Town:** keep it to at most two bubbles at a time, and only show a bubble if it is caused by a real choice the player made.
4. **One new system per stage.** Anno tiers and Frostpunk's generator add one system at a time. Eco Town's six levels already do this; each level should add exactly one new lens.
5. **Short sessions with clear endings.** Islanders and Dorfromantik runs are about 20–40 minutes.
   **Eco Town:** six levels fitted into 45, 60 or 90 minutes, with a save after each level so a class can resume next lesson.
6. **More than one way to win.** Planet Zoo combines welfare and guest ratings. In Eco Town, the town flower, with titles drawn from the strongest petals, lets very different towns succeed. The petals must visibly pull against each other, or this is just decoration.
7. **The weight of choices depends on the age band.**
   - **13–17:** Frostpunk's success (5M+) shows teenagers accept real dilemmas with no right answer, such as housing density against green space, a car ban against drivers' jobs, or a wind farm against a view. Give the older version at least one of these per level.
   - **8–12:** follow Townscaper and Terra Nil: no fail screen, gentle consequences.
8. **Restoring nature can be the win itself.** Terra Nil and Timberborn put ecology at the centre of the mechanics.
   **Eco Town:** wildlife returning is the clearest visible reward. It already appears in DESIGN.md and should be made louder.
9. **Showing your town to others.** Animal Crossing island visits and Minecraft showcases drive these games' spread.
   **Eco Town:** a class gallery or tour on the facilitator's device and a printed town report, with no accounts or online sharing (safeguarding).
10. **No in-game purchases, and nothing needs to be online.** Townscaper, Islanders and Dorfromantik are premium games with no in-app purchases. Roblox-style currencies are a problem for schools buying the game. Eco Town should run in a browser on low-end Chromebooks, with no login or network needed.

### What the education evidence adds

- The Eco pilot found better attitudes and systems thinking, but **no gain in content knowledge**. A game on its own does not teach facts.
  **Eco Town:** keep the facilitator debriefs and the end-of-lab discussion in `LAB-GUIDE.md` as required parts of the lab, not optional extras. Assess with the exit check, not with in-game stars alone.
- Block by Block works because the output is real: young people's designs are built.
  **Eco Town:** the town report should be usable as a starting point for a local consultation, for example "walk your street and bring evidence".
- SimCityEDU's lasting lesson is the format: short, structured missions built around one pollution-versus-economy dilemma, with teacher-facing data. It fits Eco Town's levels. Its outcome evidence was never published, so cite it as a design precedent only.

## 3. What to avoid, with evidence

| Risk | What happened | Eco Town rule |
|---|---|---|
| Needing an online connection | SimCity (2013) required a connection, and server failures made it unplayable at launch. It is widely blamed for ending the series ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/simcity-launched-a-decade-ago-and-it-was-so-disastrous-it-killed-the-series/)) | The game works fully offline. All libraries are vendored and there is no login |
| Small maps and agents that fake their behaviour | SimCity (2013) was criticised for small plots and simulated people who did not really behave as individuals ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/opinion-i-simcity-i-launch-debacle-comes-down-to-mismanaged-expectations)) | The simulation must be honest and consistent. Only the AI helper is allowed to be wrong, and the lenses always tell the truth |
| Poor performance at launch | Cities: Skylines II launched with performance problems and mixed Steam reviews. Its CEO later said the launch was "rightfully overshadowed" by technical issues ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/cities-skylines-ii-ceo-no-longer-afraid-of-the-marketing-department-admits-the-city-builders-launch-was-rightfully-overshadowed-by-technical-issues)). It sold 1M in about 3 months, against the first game's 1M in about a month ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-has-sold-one-million-copies)) | Set a performance budget for a low-end Chromebook (30 fps target), cap voxel counts, keep post-processing optional |
| A sequel that loses what the original had | Frostpunk 2 sold 592K by end-2024, against the first game's 5M+ | Keep the core idea, the confidently wrong AI caught from the ground, at the centre of both age versions. Don't let the 13–17 version turn into a spreadsheet |
| Platform currencies and open chat | Roblox's age mix and moderation make it hard to adopt in schools | No currency, no chat, no accounts, no collection of personal data |

## 4. Implications for the two versions

| | Ages 8–12 | Ages 13–17 |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark feel | Animal Crossing, Townscaper | Cities: Skylines, Frostpunk, with Roblox and Fortnite polish expectations |
| Lenses | One at a time, with icons and patterns | Combined, in real units, with a trade-off chart |
| Choices | Gentle, with no failure | At least one real dilemma per level with no single right answer |
| The AI helper | Choose the clearer instruction card | Write the instruction from goal, limit and check; judge whether its stated confidence is justified |
| Evidence | Stars for spotting mistakes | An evidence log, plus a sampling-bias check on residents' feedback |

## 5. Gaps and figures to check before external use

- PEGI/ESRB ratings for every title except Minecraft (PEGI 7), Animal Crossing (PEGI 3) and Frostpunk (PEGI 16).
- Unit sales for Anno 1800, Islanders, Tropico, Banished, Pocket City and the RollerCoaster Tycoon series; none were found from reliable sources.
- A current official Minecraft Education user figure.
- Learning-outcome studies for Minecraft Education and Cities: Skylines in classrooms. Neither was reviewed here.
- The SRI evaluation of SimCityEDU, if it was ever published.

## 6. Corrections to the first draft

The first draft was a single-pass agent summary. Checking it claim by claim found:

- **Eco (Strange Loop)** was listed as "no sales disclosed". The US Department of Education page says 400K+ copies on Steam. The draft also left out the pilot results, including the null result on content knowledge, which matters most for Eco Town's design.
- **Minecraft Education's** "2M users" is from 2017 and was presented without noting how old it is.
- **Townscaper's** 500K is a 2021 figure. The draft didn't mention newer estimates.
- **Big sellers next to the genre** (Stardew Valley 41M+, Terraria 70M) were missing, as were Block by Block and the Roblox 13–17 audience data. The audience data is directly relevant to the older version.
- **SimCityEDU** was presented as a model with an "about 3,000 data points per player" claim. That claim was not re-verified here, and no outcome results were found, so it is now framed as a design precedent only.
- **Cities: Skylines II**: the "51% negative reviews" claim couldn't be confirmed and has been softened to "mixed".
- **Estimates and official figures** were mixed in one ranked table. They are now separated, and every figure is tagged by type.
- **"About $500 for a 100-seat pack" for Eco** had no source and has been removed.
