**Reviewer notes:**
- Render status: Described from the current source; video re-rendering. Timings come from `rocket/index.html` and `rocket/audio.json`; eight stills rendered from the current source (at 2, 6, 11, 15, 19, 24, 30 and 34 seconds) were checked for layout and wording. Check the finished video against this list.
- This is a full rewrite: the trailer is now a single drone-ship level (37.6 s). The hop test (Level 1), the 31 m/s bounce and the Mars level have all been removed.
- Fact to check, and the biggest one: a returning booster cannot wait in the air for 20 minutes. The wall says "BOOSTER ON THE WAY HOME" and the children then call "HOLD" and land "20 MINUTES LATER". Real boosters land within about ten minutes of launch with only seconds of fuel to spare. Consider rewording so the hold happens before launch (for example "hold the launch until the wind drops") or making clear this is a game simplification.
- Facts to check: the 45 km/h gust and "Waves: 3 m" as limits for a drone-ship landing; "T+ 00:08:40" as the time a booster is on its way back (about right for real landings).
- The level banner says "LEVEL 4 OF 6" and stars start at 4, but the trailer shows no other level. Fine for a trailer, but viewers may wonder what the other five are.
- The clock in the launch panel only runs from "T+00:00" to "T+00:02" before the cut, so it reads as a real-time clock; the mission control wall then jumps to "T+ 00:08:40" without a "later" caption.
- The launch caption "What goes up has to come back down." is on screen for under a second.
- Stars go 4 → 5 (the HOLD call) → 6 (the landing), but three big stars appear in the sky after the landing; check that viewers do not read this as "3 stars".
- NAV's confidence goes down from 89% (the wrong plan) to 72% (the checked plan). That is a deliberate point (the cautious plan is less certain) but a reviewer might see it as odd.
- The NAV card at 0:07–0:11 and the wind graph carry small text; the key numbers (12 km/h, 45 km/h) are readable, the axis labels on a phone are not.
- Job titles are real; "GNC engineer" (guidance, navigation and control) is jargon for ages 8–14. Mission-control roles on the wall ("FLIGHT", "GUIDANCE", "PROPULSION", "WEATHER") are tiny and decorative.
