# <Lab name>: design document

> Strand: <Client request / Brochure world-building / Career skills / Future Skills / First concept> · Ages: <x–y> · Session: 45 / 60 / 90 min · Trailer: `games/trailers/<id>/` (`out/<id>.mp4`)

## 1. Summary
Two or three sentences: what children do, what the AI gets wrong, and what they learn.

## 2. Learning outcomes
- 4–6 outcomes, each starting "By the end, learners can…" and written to be observable.
- Curriculum links (England KS2/KS3/KS4, plus NGSS where relevant) and frameworks (UNESCO AI competencies, AI4K12 Big Ideas).

## 3. Real-world connection and careers
- Which real jobs this mirrors, and what those people actually do.
- How professionals use AI in this work today.

## 4. Setting, look and characters
- Visual style (voxel diorama, pixel art, workplace…). The AI helper's name, appearance and voice.
- Fictional names used. All of them must be checked so they don't match a real organisation or person.

## 5. Core loop and structure
- The act structure used in every level or round.
- A table of levels/rounds: goal · real decisions (with the options) · the AI's confident mistake (instruction → guess with % sure) · how children catch it (evidence, lens or test) · the clearer instruction or better data · what changes on screen.
- At least one "the AI was unsure but right" moment.

## 6. Team roles
Three to five rotating roles: what each does and the evidence or tool each one holds.

## 7. Session plans
- 45-minute, 60-minute and 90-minute versions as minute-by-minute tables: warm-up, play, discussion, wrap-up.
- What to cut when you are short of time.

## 8. Assessment
- How success is judged: stars or catches, artefacts, an exit check with three questions, and a facilitator observation rubric with four criteria and three levels each.

## 9. Discussion session (instructor-led, at the end of the lab)
Plan 10–20 minutes, depending on session length. Include:
- **Purpose:** the big idea the discussion should land.
- **Set-up:** room layout, materials, timing, and how to bring the class back from screens.
- **Structure:** for example think–pair–share → whole-group → personal takeaway, with minutes for each step.
- **Opening question:** one that everyone can answer.
- **Core questions:** 6–10 questions, each with:
  - its purpose;
  - what a strong answer sounds like;
  - a follow-up probe.

  Mix the types: recall of what happened, reasoning about why the AI went wrong, transfer to real life, ethics and fairness, and careers.
- **Sentence starters** for learners, for example "The AI assumed… because…" and "I would check… by…".
- **Common misconceptions** to listen for, and how to respond to each.
- **Differentiation:** prompts for younger or less confident learners, and stretch prompts for older or confident ones.
- **Connecting to real life:** one current, age-appropriate real-world example to discuss. Describe it so it stays accurate; don't invent statistics.
- **Closing ritual:** a one-sentence takeaway each, plus the "next step" pathway.
- **Facilitator notes:** sensitive topics, safeguarding signposting, and managing dominant voices.

## 10. Accessibility and inclusion
Reading level, audio and captions, colour-blind-safe signals, motor-friendly controls, and how to run the lab with no devices at home.

## 11. Safety, ethics and data
- What data is collected (ideally none).
- Rules on age and real AI tools.
- Consequences are kept gentle.

## 12. Build notes
- Scenes and assets needed, a list of scripted AI responses, what can be reused from the trailer, and open questions.
