# Creative Producer: design document

> Strand: Future Skills · Ages: 11–16 (default; scripted, pre-generated drafts at 11–13; optional live generation at 14–16) · Session: 45 / 60 / 90 min · Trailer: `games/trailers/fs-creative/` (`out/fs-creative.mp4`, not yet rendered)

## 1. Summary

Learners are creative producers making a poster for the Harbourside Lantern Festival, with an AI-use statement. They write a creative spec with rights rules, start from their own sketch, take an AI colleague's draft, then review it. The draft is 93% sure and "finished", but its lanterns were redrawn in the style of a living illustrator from 40 of her portfolio images, and it calls a harbour photo "royalty-free" when the licence says non-commercial only, while the festival sells tickets. Learners learn that making with AI becomes professional work when you can show what you made, what the AI made, where everything came from and that you are allowed to use it. Credit is not permission.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end, learners can…
- write a creative spec with audience, tone, accessibility and at least three rights rules;
- read a licence label (own work, CC BY, CC BY-NC, Open Font Licence, "royalty-free") and say whether it allows this use;
- write a full credit for a Creative Commons image (title, author, source, licence, and any changes);
- spot when an AI output depends on one living artist's work without permission, and replace it;
- check every fact on the poster against the client's source;
- write an honest AI-use statement that says what the AI did and what they did.

## 3. Real-world connection and careers

- **Jobs this mirrors:** content producer, graphic designer, marketing assistant, games artist, museum interpretation officer, sound designer. They work from a client brief, source or make images, clear rights and licences, keep a credits log, check facts with the client and get sign-off before anything is printed.
- **How professionals use AI in this work today:** for mood boards, layout and colour options and quick drafts. Studios set rules on approved tools, what may be fed in, no imitating named living artists, and disclosure to the client.
- **Facilitator reference: UK rights, stated carefully** (not legal advice)

  | Point | What to say |
  |---|---|
  | Your own work | Copyright arises automatically when you make an original work; no registration is needed (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988). Leo owns the copyright in his drawing |
  | Style | Copyright protects a work's expression, not a general style or idea. Copying a substantial part of a specific work can infringe. Whether a particular "in the style of" output crosses that line depends on the facts (to verify for any real case). Separately, the client's rule bans naming living artists, and using 40 of one artist's images without permission is unfair to her |
  | Training AI on others' work | Whether training on copyright works without permission is lawful in the UK is unsettled. The text-and-data-mining exception (s.29A CDPA) covers non-commercial research only. The government consulted on changes in 2024–25 (to verify current position) |
  | AI-generated output | UK law has a provision for "computer-generated works" with no human author (s.9(3) CDPA), and the government consulted on whether to keep it (to verify). Whether a purely AI-made image is protected is uncertain (to verify) |
  | CC BY 4.0 | Allows commercial use if you credit the creator, name and link the licence, and say if you changed it ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)) |
  | CC BY-NC | Non-commercial use only. A poster for a ticketed festival is very likely commercial, so ask the rights holder or choose another image (borderline cases (to verify)) |
  | "Royalty-free" | Means you don't pay per use under a licence. It does not mean copyright-free or free for any use. Read the licence |
  | Open Font Licence | Fonts under the SIL Open Font Licence can be used in printed and commercial designs; the font itself can't be sold on its own |
  | Logos | Usually registered trade marks and often copyright. Including one can suggest the brand endorses you. Leave them out unless you have permission |
  | Crediting someone falsely | Don't label work as made by someone who didn't make it (false attribution, s.84 CDPA) (to verify) |
  | AI-use statements | There is no general UK legal duty for an individual to label AI-assisted images (to verify). Clients, platforms and exam boards set their own rules. The ASA's advertising rules apply to ads however they were made (to verify) |

- **Mentor video:** Sam Okafor, graphic designer: "Every picture needs a credit." It counts as an employer encounter only if a real designer records it ([Gatsby](https://gatsbybenchmarks.org.uk/?p=777)).

**The AI was unsure but right (stage 4).** The colleague flags: "The white date text on the orange sky may be hard to read. Not sure (55%)." Learners darken the band behind the date.

## 8. Assessment

- **Artefact quality and catches.** Catches: the "credit is enough" rule (stage 1), the copied living artist and the wrong licence (stage 3), the wrong day and the logo (stage 4). "Secure" is 3 of 5. Artefact quality checklist for the Production Pack: the design meets the spec; every element has a credit and licence; the CC BY credit is complete; facts match the brief; the AI-use statement is honest and specific; alt text written; contrast passes.
- **Exit check**
  1. "An image says 'royalty-free'. Can you use it on a ticketed event poster?" (Model answer: not without reading the licence; royalty-free doesn't mean free for any use.)
  2. "What four things go in a Creative Commons credit?" (Model answer: title, author, source and licence, plus any changes.)
  3. "Write one honest sentence about how you used AI today." (Model answer: names what the AI did and what you did.)

**Facilitator observation rubric**

| Criterion | Developing | Secure | Ready for next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rights | Thinks a credit makes any image usable | Reads licence labels and replaces unusable items | Explains why non-commercial fails here and what to ask the rights holder |
| Fairness to creators | Sees style copying as harmless | Replaces the copied style with own work | Distinguishes being inspired by many artists from imitating one |
| Facts and access | Trusts the draft's text | Checks date and place against the brief | Adds alt text and fixes contrast unprompted |
| Honesty and ownership | "AI made it" or no statement | Specific statement of what AI and human did | Adapts the statement for different readers (client, exam board, public) |

## 9. Discussion session (instructor-led, at the end of the lab)

Plan 10 minutes (45-minute session), 15 minutes (60) or 20 minutes (90).

- **Purpose:** making with AI is professional when you can show what you made, what the AI made, where everything came from and that you're allowed to use it. Credit is not permission. "Royalty-free" is not "free for anything". The person who signs the work owns the result.
- **Set-up:** call "Pens down, posters up". Each team props its before/after print on the table; chairs turn in a horseshoe. On the board: the trailer's "Before" card (two labelled problem boxes) beside the "After", the Credits & licences panel, and the Four Checks poster. Each learner has three cards: *Can use* (green ✓), *Can't use* (✕) and *Ask first* (?), each with a word and a symbol. Step out of role: "I'm not your client any more."
- **Structure**

  | Step | 10 min | 15 min | 20 min |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Opening question (card vote) | 1 | 2 | 2 |
  | Think–pair (Q1–Q2) | 2 | 3 | 4 |
  | Whole group (choose from Q3–Q9; always include Q4 and Q6) | 4 | 6 | 8 |
  | Real-life example | 1 | 2 | 3 |
  | Closing ritual and pathway | 2 | 2 | 3 |

- **Opening question:** "Hold up a card. A photo you found online, with the photographer's name under it, for our ticketed festival poster: can use, can't use, or ask first?" Then reveal: it depends on the licence, so *Ask first* (or read the licence) is the professional answer.
- **Core questions**

  1. **Recall.** "What two problems did the review find, and what did you change?" *Purpose:* name the checks. *Strong answer:* "Data: the lanterns copied Mira Kell's style from 40 of her images, so we used Leo's own drawing. Authority: the photo was non-commercial only and the festival sells tickets, so we used a CC BY photo and credited it." *Probe:* "Which check caught each one?"
  2. **Reasoning (Authority).** "The AI said 'royalty-free' and was 93% sure. Why was it wrong?" *Purpose:* labels need reading, not trusting. *Strong answer:* "Royalty-free means you don't pay each time, not that you can use it for anything. The actual licence said non-commercial, and selling tickets makes it commercial." *Probe:* "Where on a real image site would you find the licence?"
  3. **Reasoning (Data and fairness).** "What's the difference between being inspired by artists and what the AI did with Mira Kell's work?" *Purpose:* fairness to creators. *Strong answer:* "Artists learn from lots of people and add their own ideas. The AI was given one living artist's portfolio to imitate her look without asking her. She could lose work if people can get 'her style' for free." *Probe:* "Copyright protects artworks, not a general style. So why did our client still ban it?"
  4. **Professional review.** "You're a studio producer checking a junior's AI-assisted social media post for a local café. One question per check." *Purpose:* the Four Checks as a creative sign-off routine. *Strong answer:* "**Data:** what was the AI given, and whose work is in there? **Case:** does it work small on a phone, for screen-reader users, and in every format? **Source:** is every fact (price, date, address) checked with the client, and every image traced to a licence? **Authority:** do we have permission for each image, font and logo, and does the client allow AI use?" *Probe:* "Which problem would cost the café most if it went out unchecked?"
  5. **Transfer (workplace).** "In your first marketing job your manager says, 'Just grab an image off Google.' What do you say?" *Purpose:* confident, polite professional practice. *Strong answer:* "I'd say I'll find one we're allowed to use, from our own photos or one with a licence that fits, and log it in the credits so we're covered." *Probe:* "What if there's no time?"
  6. **Transfer (qualifications).** "In GCSE Art & Design or Media Studies coursework, what do you have to do if you used AI?" *Purpose:* link to exam rules. *Strong answer:* "Say clearly what AI did, keep evidence of your own process, and follow the exam board's rules. Using AI without saying so could count as malpractice." *Probe:* "Rewrite Leo's statement for a coursework portfolio. What would you add?" (Tool name, date, what was kept or changed.)
  7. **Honesty.** "Leo wrote, 'I used an AI tool for layout and colour ideas. The lanterns are my own drawing.' Is that enough? Too much?" *Purpose:* what makes a statement useful. *Strong answer:* "It's specific about what the AI did and what Leo did, and it's short enough to read. A client might also want the tool's name." *Probe:* "Would a statement saying just 'Made with AI' be honest?"
  8. **Ethics.** "Who wins and who loses if anyone can make posters by copying living artists with AI?" *Purpose:* weigh impacts. *Strong answer:* "Clients save money; people without drawing skills can make things. But artists can lose income and control over their work, and audiences can be misled about who made it." *Probe:* "What rule would be fair to everyone?"
  9. **Careers.** "Content producer, graphic designer, marketing assistant: what did you do today that they do every week?" *Purpose:* make the jobs visible. *Strong answer:* "Working to a brief, checking rights and facts, keeping a credits log, and getting sign-off." *Probe:* "What could you put in an Arts Award or iDEA portfolio from today?"

- **Transfer map**

  | Today | At work | In your next qualification |
  |---|---|---|
  | Creative spec | Client brief and style guide | Design brief in GCSE Art & Design / D&T |
  | Credits and licences panel | Rights log, image licensing | Acknowledging sources in NEA |
  | AI-use statement | Disclosure to client or publisher | JCQ AI acknowledgement in coursework |
  | Four Checks sign-off | Proof approval before print | Evaluating your own work; Arts Award review |

- **Sentence starters:** "We could / couldn't use this because the licence says…" · "The AI assumed… because…" · "I checked… against…" · "The credit needs… and…" · "What I made: … What the AI did: …" · "At work this would be called…"
- **Common misconceptions**
  - *"If it's online, it's free to use."* Almost everything online is someone's work. Look for the licence.
  - *"If I credit it, I can use it."* A credit is required by some licences, but it never replaces permission.
  - *"Royalty-free means free."* It means no per-use fee under a licence. Read what the licence allows.
  - *"AI images have no copyright, so anything goes."* The law on AI outputs is unsettled, the tool's terms still apply, and the output can still copy someone's work.
  - *"Using AI is cheating."* It depends on the rules of the task. Our brief allowed AI for layout, and we said so. In exams, follow the exam board's rules.
  - *"Copying a style is illegal."* It's more nuanced: copyright protects works, not general styles. The problems here were using her work without permission, the client's rule, and fairness.
- **Differentiation**
  - *Younger or less confident:* use the three cards for every question first; offer two-option prompts ("Own drawing or copied style: which is safer? Why?"); fill-in credits ("Photo: ___, licence: ___").
  - *Older or confident:* compare CC licences (BY, BY-SA, BY-NC, BY-ND) for three uses; draft an AI-use policy for a small studio; discuss whether training AI on artists' work without permission should be allowed, using the government consultation; look up the s.9(3) "computer-generated works" debate (to verify).
- **Connecting to real life:** between December 2024 and February 2025 the UK government consulted on copyright and AI, including whether AI developers should be allowed to train on copyright works unless creators opt out. Many artists, musicians, writers and news publishers campaigned against an opt-out approach, and the issue was debated again during the passage of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (to verify current position). Ask: if creators had to opt *in*, how would the AI's lantern draft have been different? Who should decide?
- **Closing ritual:** each learner says one sentence: "Next time I make something with AI, I will ___." Then read the pathway card (60 seconds):
  - **Next step:** iDEA creative badges (free, online) ([iDEA](https://idea.org.uk/about)).
  - **Award:** Arts Award (Explore or Bronze) ([Arts Award](https://www.artsaward.org.uk/resource/?id=4880) *[secondary]*).
  - **Route:** GCSE Art & Design or Media Studies → creative and digital marketing apprenticeship.
  - **Jobs:** content producer, graphic designer, marketing assistant. Hand out the anonymous badge code ([Open Badges 3.0](https://www.imsglobal.org/spec/ob/v3p0)).
- **Facilitator notes**
  - *Sensitive topics:* AI images of real people (fake photos, manipulated images) may come up. Acknowledge it briefly, stay general, and return to the poster. Never show examples.
  - *Safeguarding:* if a learner mentions an image of themselves or someone they know being made, changed or shared without consent, follow the school's DSL procedure. Childline's Report Remove service helps under-18s get nude images removed (to verify current details); Childline 0800 1111.
  - *Dominant voices and creative confidence:* some learners think they "can't draw". Value the sketch as ownership, not skill. Use the card vote so everyone answers; ask quieter Recorders to read their credits aloud; "three before me".

## 10. Accessibility and inclusion

- **Reading level:** about reading age 10 for 11–13 materials; licence labels in plain words with a symbol each.
- **Audio and captions:** captioned mentor video; the facilitator reads AI lines and licences aloud.
- **Colour-blind-safe signals:** ✓/✕/? plus words; poster problem boxes have text labels, not just red outlines. The poster's own contrast is checked in stage 4.
- **Motor-friendly controls:** drawing on paper (photographed by the facilitator on a school device, or traced in-tool) or a simple shape tool; 44 px targets; keyboard operation.
- **No devices at home:** the unplugged version uses printed drafts, licence cards, a paper poster and a printed credits sheet. Arts Award and iDEA both have free in-school routes.

## 11. Safety, ethics and data

- **Data collected:** none. No child accounts. Learners' drawings stay on paper or on the school's device; DeltaOps stores nothing. No photos of learners, ever.
- **Rules on age and real AI tools**
  - 11–13: scripted AI colleague and a library of pre-generated drafts only. No live generation.
  - 14–16 (optional, 90-minute version): live image generation **only** inside the school's own managed accounts, if the school's tool allows it for under-18s (to verify per tool), meeting the DfE *Generative AI: product safety expectations* ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations)), with the DSL's agreement and logging on. Prompts must follow the spec: no real people, no named living artists, no logos.
  - Museums and holiday camps without a school DSL run the 11–13 version.
- **Fair to creators:** all "Mira Kell" artwork is made by DeltaOps and labelled fictional. Pre-generated drafts are made with tools whose terms allow this use (to verify).
- **Gentle consequences:** problems are labelled boxes that turn to ticks. Nobody is told they "broke the law".
